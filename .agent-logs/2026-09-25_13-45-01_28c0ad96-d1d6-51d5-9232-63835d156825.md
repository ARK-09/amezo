## Where I didn't follow the design

The design shows things the API can't back. I built the markup but left it data-driven rather than faked:

- **Hero campaign** — it advertises "iPhone 16 Pro Max, from $1,199". There's no campaign API and the catalog has no iPhone, so each slide is a real listing with its real title, price and link.
- **Discount flashes** — `-25%` badges and struck-through was-prices. Nothing carries a compare-at price. `ProductTile` accepts one and renders neither until the API supplies it. Inventing a discount on a real listing isn't a styling detail, so I wouldn't hardcode these.
- **Rail titles and promo tiles** — "Today's best deals", "60% off winter wear", a clearance push. They now name what they actually link to.
- **The AI band** — shipped as search, because that's what it does. The "Amezo AI / AI-powered shopping experience" copy is one string in `SearchBanner.tsx` whenever something AI sits behind it. Your call — say the word and I'll put it back.
- **Left out entirely rather than shipped inert:** Follow/Message on the storefront, seller policies (returns/warranty/ships-from), the LIVE badge, and buyer "Sign in" — that slot shows the magic-link identity when checkout established one, otherwise "Sell on Amezo". Footer's Help centre / Returns / Track order need pages that don't exist. All quick once there's something behind them.

Delivery city is real — a persisted localStorage preference, not a dead control.

Two things I changed on my own judgement: the landing page gained an `sr-only` h1 (the design's layout has no room for one, and the site's home page needs it), and rail grids use `auto-fill` with a 2-up mobile fallback — the design's `auto-fit` stretched a 1-item rail into a single 1300px-wide card, and on a phone made the page 10,000px tall.
